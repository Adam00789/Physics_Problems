# Task 05 – Inelastic Collision

## Problem Statement

A $70 \text{ kg}$ runner moving at $3 \text{ m/s}$ jumps onto a $140 \text{ kg}$ stationary cart. What is the final speed of the cart with the runner? Is kinetic energy conserved in this collision? Explain.

## Theory

In an inelastic collision where two objects stick together, the total linear momentum of the system is conserved, provided no external horizontal forces act on the system:

$$
P_{initial} = P_{final}
$$

$$
m_1 v_1 + m_2 v_2 = (m_1 + m_2) v_f
$$

Kinetic energy ($KE = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$) is generally **not** conserved in inelastic collisions. The "missing" energy is transformed into internal energy, such as heat, sound, or work done during deformation.

## Step-by-Step Solution

### 1. Conservation of Momentum

Given:
* $m_1 = 70 \text{ kg}, v_1 = 3 \text{ m/s}$ (Runner)
* $m_2 = 140 \text{ kg}, v_2 = 0 \text{ m/s}$ (Cart)

$$
(70 \times 3) + (140 \times 0) = (70 + 140) v_f
$$

$$
210 = 210 v_f
$$

$$
v_f = 1 \text{ m/s}
$$

### 2. Kinetic Energy Analysis

Initial Kinetic Energy ($KE_i$):

$$
KE_i = \frac{1}{2} m_1 v_1^2 = \frac{1}{2}(70)(3^2) = 315 \text{ J}
$$

Final Kinetic Energy ($KE_f$):

$$
KE_f = \frac{1}{2} (m_1 + m_2) v_f^2 = \frac{1}{2}(210)(1^2) = 105 \text{ J}
$$

## Final Result

* The final speed of the cart and runner is **$1 \text{ m/s}$**.
* Kinetic energy is **not conserved**. The system lost $210 \text{ J}$ of mechanical energy.

## Interpretation

The runner’s momentum is distributed over a much larger total mass, resulting in a significantly lower final velocity. The loss of $2/3$ of the initial kinetic energy is characteristic of perfectly inelastic collisions, where the internal work required to synchronize the velocities of the two bodies dissipates energy.
