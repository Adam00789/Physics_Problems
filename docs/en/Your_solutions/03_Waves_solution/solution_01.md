<h1 style="color:#4fc3f7">Task 01 – Wave Properties</h1>

<h2 style="color:#81d4fa">Problem Statement</h2>

A sound wave in air has a frequency of $f = 440\ \text{Hz}$. The speed of sound in air is $v_\text{air} = 343\ \text{m/s}$. The speed of sound in water is $v_\text{water} = 1482\ \text{m/s}$.

Find the wavelength of the wave in air and in water.

---

<h2 style="color:#81d4fa">Theory</h2>

All mechanical and electromagnetic waves satisfy the fundamental relation between wave speed $v$, frequency $f$, and wavelength $\lambda$:

$$
v = f \lambda
$$

Rearranging for wavelength:

$$
\lambda = \frac{v}{f}
$$

The frequency $f$ is a property of the source and does not change as the wave passes from one medium to another. Only the wave speed — and hence the wavelength — changes with the medium.

---

<h2 style="color:#81d4fa">Step-by-Step Solution</h2>

<h3 style="color:#b3e5fc">Wavelength in Air</h3>

Given:

- $f = 440\ \text{Hz}$
- $v_\text{air} = 343\ \text{m/s}$

$$
\lambda_\text{air} = \frac{v_\text{air}}{f} = \frac{343}{440}\ \text{m}
$$

$$
\lambda_\text{air} \approx 0.780\ \text{m}
$$

<h3 style="color:#b3e5fc">Wavelength in Water</h3>

Given:

- $f = 440\ \text{Hz}$ (unchanged — determined by the source, not the medium)
- $v_\text{water} = 1482\ \text{m/s}$

$$
\lambda_\text{water} = \frac{v_\text{water}}{f} = \frac{1482}{440}\ \text{m}
$$

$$
\lambda_\text{water} \approx 3.368\ \text{m}
$$

---

<h2 style="color:#81d4fa">Final Result</h2>

$$
\lambda_\text{air} \approx 0.780\ \text{m}
$$

$$
\lambda_\text{water} \approx 3.368\ \text{m}
$$

---

<h2 style="color:#81d4fa">Interpretation</h2>

The same 440 Hz tone has a wavelength roughly 4.3 times larger in water than in air. This follows directly from the higher propagation speed: sound travels through water about 4.3 times faster because water is far less compressible than air, making the restoring forces stronger and the wave speed higher. The frequency — set by the source — remains constant across both media.
