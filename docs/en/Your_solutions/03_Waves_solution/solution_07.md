<h1 style="color:#4fc3f7">Task 07 – Standing Wave Modes</h1>

<h2 style="color:#81d4fa">Problem Statement</h2>

A standing wave with four antinodes is produced on a string of length $L = 80\ \text{cm} = 0.80\ \text{m}$. Determine the wavelength of this wave.

---

<h2 style="color:#81d4fa">Theory</h2>

A string fixed at both ends supports standing waves whose endpoints are nodes. The $n$-th harmonic has $n$ antinodes and $n+1$ nodes (including both fixed ends). The length of the string must accommodate exactly $n$ half-wavelengths:

$$
L = n \cdot \frac{\lambda_n}{2}
$$

Solving for the wavelength of the $n$-th mode:

$$
\lambda_n = \frac{2L}{n}
$$

The number of antinodes equals the harmonic number $n$.

---

<h2 style="color:#81d4fa">Step-by-Step Solution</h2>

<h3 style="color:#b3e5fc">Step 1 — Identify the Harmonic Number</h3>

Four antinodes correspond to the fourth harmonic:

$$
n = 4
$$

<h3 style="color:#b3e5fc">Step 2 — Apply the Wavelength Formula</h3>

$$
\lambda_4 = \frac{2L}{n} = \frac{2 \times 0.80\ \text{m}}{4}
$$

$$
\lambda_4 = \frac{1.60}{4}\ \text{m}
$$

$$
\lambda_4 = 0.40\ \text{m}
$$

---

<h2 style="color:#81d4fa">Final Result</h2>

$$
\lambda_4 = 0.40\ \text{m}
$$

---

<h2 style="color:#81d4fa">Interpretation</h2>

The fourth harmonic has a wavelength of 40 cm. The string of 80 cm accommodates exactly two full wavelengths (four half-wavelengths), producing four antinodes and five nodes. As a check: $L = 4 \times (\lambda_4/2) = 4 \times 0.20\ \text{m} = 0.80\ \text{m}$. In comparison, the fundamental ($n=1$) would have $\lambda_1 = 2 \times 0.80 = 1.60\ \text{m}$, four times longer.
