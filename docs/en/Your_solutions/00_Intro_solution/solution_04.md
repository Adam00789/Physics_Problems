# Task 04 – Rearranging Formulas

## Problem Statement

The period of a simple pendulum is given by

$$
T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{L}{g}}
$$

Rearrange the equation to obtain a formula for $g$.

---

## Theory

Rearranging formulas involves isolating the desired variable using algebraic operations.

Key steps include:

- eliminating square roots by squaring both sides  
- isolating variables through multiplication or division  

---

## Step-by-Step Solution

Start from the original equation:

$$
T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{L}{g}}
$$

---

### Step 1: Isolate the square root

Divide both sides by $2\pi$:

$$
\frac{T}{2\pi} = \sqrt{\frac{L}{g}}
$$

---

### Step 2: Eliminate the square root

Square both sides:

$$
\left(\frac{T}{2\pi}\right)^2 = \frac{L}{g}
$$

---

### Step 3: Solve for $g$

Rearrange:

$$
g = \frac{L}{\left(\frac{T}{2\pi}\right)^2}
$$

---

### Step 4: Simplify the expression

$$
\left(\frac{T}{2\pi}\right)^2 = \frac{T^2}{4\pi^2}
$$

Substitute:

$$
g = \frac{L}{\frac{T^2}{4\pi^2}}
$$

---

Invert the denominator:

$$
g = L \cdot \frac{4\pi^2}{T^2}
$$

---

## Final Result

$$
g = \frac{4\pi^2 L}{T^2}
$$

---

## Interpretation

- The acceleration due to gravity is directly proportional to the pendulum length $L$.  
- It is inversely proportional to the square of the period $T^2$.  
- This formula is commonly used in experiments to determine $g$ by measuring $T$ and $L$.
