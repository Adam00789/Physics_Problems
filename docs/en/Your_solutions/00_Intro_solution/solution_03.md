# Task 03 – Proportionality in Gravitation

## Problem Statement

The gravitational force is given by

$$
F = G \frac{m_1 m_2}{r^2}
$$

Determine how $F$ changes if:

- $r$ is doubled  
- $m_1$ and $m_2$ are both halved  

---

## Theory

The force depends on variables proportionally:

$$
F \propto \frac{m_1 m_2}{r^2}
$$

Changes in variables can be analyzed using scaling factors.

---

## Step-by-Step Solution

Let the original force be:

$$
F = G \frac{m_1 m_2}{r^2}
$$

---

### Apply changes

New masses:

$$
m_1' = \frac{m_1}{2}, \qquad m_2' = \frac{m_2}{2}
$$

New distance:

$$
r' = 2r
$$

---

### Substitute into the formula

$$
F' = G \frac{m_1' m_2'}{(r')^2}
$$

$$
F' = G \frac{\left(\frac{m_1}{2}\right)\left(\frac{m_2}{2}\right)}{(2r)^2}
$$

---

### Simplify numerator

$$
\left(\frac{m_1}{2}\right)\left(\frac{m_2}{2}\right) = \frac{m_1 m_2}{4}
$$

---

### Simplify denominator

$$
(2r)^2 = 4r^2
$$

---

### Combine results

$$
F' = G \frac{\frac{m_1 m_2}{4}}{4r^2}
$$

$$
F' = G \frac{m_1 m_2}{16 r^2}
$$

---

### Compare with original force

$$
F = G \frac{m_1 m_2}{r^2}
$$

$$
F' = \frac{1}{16} F
$$

---

## Final Result

$$
F' = \frac{1}{16} F
$$

---

## Interpretation

- Doubling the distance reduces the force by a factor of $4$.  
- Halving both masses reduces the force by another factor of $4$.  
- The combined effect reduces the gravitational force by a factor of $16$.  

The force becomes significantly weaker due to its strong dependence on distance and linear dependence on each mass.
