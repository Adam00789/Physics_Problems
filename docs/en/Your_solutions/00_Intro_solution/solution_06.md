# Task 06 – Function Analysis

## Problem Statement

Consider the function

$$
f(x) = 3x^2 - 12x + 7
$$

Identify any local maxima or minima.

---

## Theory

A quadratic function

$$
f(x) = ax^2 + bx + c
$$

has a single extremum (maximum or minimum) at its vertex.

The $x$-coordinate of the vertex is given by

$$
x_v = -\frac{b}{2a}
$$

- If $a > 0$, the parabola opens upward → **minimum**  
- If $a < 0$, the parabola opens downward → **maximum**

---

## Step-by-Step Solution

Given:

$$
a = 3, \quad b = -12, \quad c = 7
$$

---

### Step 1: Find the vertex position

$$
x_v = -\frac{b}{2a}
$$

$$
x_v = -\frac{-12}{2 \cdot 3}
$$

$$
x_v = \frac{12}{6}
$$

$$
x_v = 2
$$

---

### Step 2: Evaluate the function at $x = 2$

$$
f(2) = 3(2)^2 - 12(2) + 7
$$

$$
f(2) = 3 \cdot 4 - 24 + 7
$$

$$
f(2) = 12 - 24 + 7
$$

$$
f(2) = -5
$$

---

### Step 3: Determine the type of extremum

Since:

$$
a = 3 > 0
$$

the parabola opens upward, so the vertex is a **minimum**.

---

## Final Result

The function has a local minimum at:

$$
x = 2
$$

$$
f(2) = -5
$$

---

## Interpretation

- The function reaches its lowest value at $x = 2$.  
- The point $(2, -5)$ is the vertex of the parabola.  
- Since the parabola opens upward, there is no local maximum.
